# LEAVE THE LIGHT ON — story bible

*A first-person psychological horror game about a missed phone call.*

## One-line
Mara Linden drives to her dead father's lake house on the night her brother went missing, to find him.
He has been gone for six years. The thing hunting her through the dark has been looking for him the whole time too.

## The truth (what actually happened)
* **Mara Linden** (34 now, 28 then) — field recordist. Records "quiet places" for public radio. A perfectionist.
  Lets calls go to voicemail while she works; always calls back "later". Wrote off her brother's comics as "a hobby" the week before.
* **Jonah "Jo" Linden** (22 at death) — her brother, eight years younger. Mara half-raised him after their mother died (he was 9, she 17).
  Funny, soft-spoken, always in the yellow raincoat their father bought so they could find him in fog. Obsessed with owls (can do five calls).
  Draws a comic, *Captain Owl*. Makes pancakes he insists are shaped like Ohio. Leaves four-minute voicemails because "text is for cowards".
  Ends every call: **"Call me back. No rush."** He was writing a song for her birthday — seven notes, no ending. He couldn't find the last one.
* **Walter Linden** — their father. Sound engineer. Taught them to listen. Built the lodge and the ice house at Halden Lake.
  Walked them onto the ice every February at -20 to hear it sing. *"It isn't breaking, it's stretching. Everything's singing if you wait."*
* **Feb 11, six years ago, 3:12 AM.** Mara is in a city recording booth, mid-take. Her phone buzzes: JO. She turns it face-down. *Later.*
  Jo is on the ice at the north bay: the lake is singing and he wants her to hear it for her birthday (Feb 12). He leaves a 4:07 voicemail. He asks her to
  **leave the porch light on** so he can find the lodge walking back along the shore. He goes a little further for a cleaner take. The ice goes quiet.
* Mara saw the voicemail at 8 AM, listened to ten seconds, smiled, and saved the rest to listen to when she could *really listen*. At 11 AM the sheriff called.
  **She has never listened to the whole message.** She believes it contains his fear. It contains a gift.
* She searched for three days with the town. Then alone. She kept signing in at the search tent for forty days.
  Every Feb 11 she drives back, and the night plays out, and something turns her around. She wakes by the fire with the porch light on. She has done this six times.
  *Tonight is the first time she gets through.*

## The rules of the world
* **The Searcher** — a tall, wet, hooded figure in a grey-green parka with a brass lantern. Calls "Jo…" in a voice stretched thin.
  It is **the part of Mara that never stopped searching**, and the part that is terrified she'll reach the lake and have to know.
  * It does not enter lit places. *Light is how Jo finds home.* Lit rooms and lit lamps are safe.
  * It wants to bring her **back to the house** (back to waiting). When it catches her she doesn't die — she wakes by the fire. The loop.
  * It has her gloves (yellow repair stitch, right index), her boots (same tread), her coat, and her voice (the search tape).
  * In mirrors, where the player's reflection should be, it stands wearing her hood.
* **The lodge holds the night.** Memories overwrite it: warm, clean, alive — then decay back. Memories are soft, golden, slightly wrong. Voices are muffled
  (you remember the shape of a voice, not the sound).
* **The lake sings.** At first it is the most frightening sound in the game. By the end it is the most beautiful.

## What the player thinks vs. what is true
| Player believes | Truth | Seeded by |
|---|---|---|
| Jo went missing last night | Six years ago | stuck calendar, mail, dust, dead truck, search sign-in sheet, tape label |
| The Searcher is hunting me | It is searching *with* me / trying to take me home | its call = Mara's voice on the tape; gloves; boots; mirror; "keep the lights on" note in Mara's own hand |
| The cracking ice is a threat | It's the lake singing | Dad's tape: "it isn't breaking, it's stretching" |
| The voicemail is a plea | It's a birthday present | Ch.3 partial playback; post-credit ending |
| I'm trying to survive | I'm trying to say goodbye | everything |

## Chapters
0. **FEB 11** — wake in the car, dead road, snowfall, porch light on the hill. First glimpse: a lantern in the trees. *Tutorial by doing: look, walk, flashlight, breath.*
1. **HALDEN LODGE** — a house kept exactly as it was. Warm coat dripping. The phone. Kitchen memory (Jo cooking; he hides something). Jo's room. The mirror.
   Wardrobe hide while the Searcher walks the house. Puzzles: cookie-jar key (observation), piano owls (music), Dad's tin (dates & temperatures).
2. **THE SEARCH** — forest trail, ribbons, lamp posts. The search tent: sign-in sheet (112 names → 4 → one). The tape of Mara calling. Lamp-to-lamp stealth. Aurora over the lake.
3. **THE ICE HOUSE** — Dad's listening room above, ice cellars and brick tunnels below. Assemble the voicemail from tape fragments. Hear most of it. The tape is *years* old.
   The lights die; the search tape plays. Pursuit through tunnels to the lodge cellar.
4. **THE LOOP** — the lodge as it was on the birthday: party set, balloons, impossible hallway. Close your eyes and follow the hum. The booth: you reach for the phone; your hand passes through.
5. **SUMMER** — a playable memory. Sun, loon, canoe, Jo. Record the lake with him. The origin of "no rush". He hums the song; it isn't finished. Sunset; ice crack.
6. **THIN ICE** — the frozen lake under aurora. Listen: the lower the tone, the thicker the ice. The Searcher on the shore. The north bay.
7. **GOODBYE** — thaw. Sunrise. The dock. Jo. The conversation. Hold his hand; let go when you're ready. The Searcher puts the lantern down.
   Walk up to the lodge in sunlight. The porch light is on. Peel the tape off the switch. Turn it off. The gift. Fade. Credits. Post-credit voicemail.

## The motif ("Jo's tune")
Seven notes in D Dorian, ending on an unresolved scale degree. It appears as a music box, a hum, radio static under the dial, the ice's glissandi, the piano owls.
In the ending Mara answers with the eighth note — a rising "question" — and the full theme resolves for the first time.
`D4 F4 A4 G4 F4 E4 D4  …  (E4 ↑)` — the last note is Mara's: it goes up, like a question.

## Final dialogue (draft; short on purpose)
> **Jo:** Hey. You're late.  *(beat)* Kidding. No rush.
> **Mara:** I didn't pick up.
> **Jo:** I know.
> **Mara:** You called and I —
> **Jo:** Mar. You picked up every other time.
> **Mara:** I was going to call you back.
> **Jo:** I know. That's what you do.
> **Mara:** I kept the light on.
> **Jo:** I saw it. Every year. You can turn it off now. I'm home.
> *…*
> **Jo:** Did you ever hear the end of it? The song.  **Mara** hums the seven notes, then one more, going up.
> **Jo:** *(laughs)* A question. Of course it's a question.
> **Mara:** Happy birthday, Jo. *(wrong way round — it's hers)*
> **Jo:** I'm still twenty-two. That's going to be so annoying for you.
> **Jo:** Okay. I should go.   *[hold to let go]*
> **Jo:** Call me back sometime. … No rush.
